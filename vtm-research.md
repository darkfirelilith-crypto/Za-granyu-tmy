## 2. Blood Bond (Vinculum / Узы крови)

**Source:** VTM V5 Corebook p.233–234; VTM Wiki (paradoxwikis) Blood Bond; Blood-Stained Love (2024) p.11–12; Camarilla sourcebook p.95.

### 2.1 Formation

The Blood Bond forms when a vampire (the **regnant**) feeds their **vitae** directly from their body to another being (the **thrall**). The blood must be consumed fresh — if vitae is allowed to stagnate (even a few minutes), it loses the bonding property. Three separate drinks on **three separate nights** are required to complete a full bond. Each additional drink deepens or refreshes the bond.

- One drink per night maximum advances the bond.
- Drinking one's own sire's blood at the Embrace doesn't trigger a bond (the Embrace creates a single "sire bond" Stage 1 effect that is one-directional and weaker — see below).
- A thrall may have only **one regnant** at a time; once bound at Stage 3, they are immune to being bonded to another (Stage 3 trumps/replaces other bonds).
- A regnant can maintain bonds to a number of thralls equal to their Blood Potency (per V5 alternative rules in later sourcebooks).

### 2.2 The Three Stages

| Stage | Effect on Thrall |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Stage 1 (one drink)** | **Infatuation.** Strong intermittent feelings toward the regnant — usually positive, sometimes negative. Comparable to the sire-bond felt by a fledgling. **No hard mechanical effect** but should be role-played: thrall daydreams about regnant, seeks their presence, is favorably disposed toward them. |
| **Stage 2 (two drinks)** | **Devotion.** The bond starts to influence the bound vampire's behaviour. The regnant can influence the thrall more easily; the thrall treats the regnant as their most important person. The thrall **must spend a Willpower point** to take directly harmful actions against the regnant. Otherwise they are free to act, but the regnant is a permanent fixture in their unlife. |
| **Stage 3 (three drinks)** | **Enslavement.** The thrall is completely bound to the regnant — what they call "love" is in truth an obsessive addiction. All other connections fade in comparison. The regnant becomes the most important thing in the thrall's world. The regnant may use **Dominate without eye contact** on the thrall. Low-Willpower characters will do virtually anything for their master, including risking their unlives. The thrall **cannot be blood-bonded to anyone else** while this bond holds. |

### 2.3 Breaking / Withstanding the Bond

- **Without reinforcement:** A bond fades by **one stage per month** in which the thrall does not consume the regnant's vitae (V5 Corebook default rule).
- **Resisting a direct order:** Thrall may spend **Willpower** to act against the regnant — short-term resistance for direct opposition, longer for indirect opposition.
- **Total seclusion:** Months of complete isolation from the regnant will degrade and finally break the bond.
- **Regnant's Final Death:** A more sudden method; breaks the bond but can have dangerous side effects (e.g., grief-fueled Wassail / frenzy risk; some thralls seek a new regnant).
- **Alternative "defiance test" rule (In Memoriam p.20–21 + Gehenna War p.44, optional):** Bond Strength is rated 1–6 (one per drink) and decreases by 1 per month without drinking. To attempt something against the regnant's wishes, thrall makes **Resolve + Intelligence** vs current Bond Strength. Once per turn when regnant is present, once per scene when not present. To fully break, Bond Strength must reach 0. (This is the optional expanded model.)
- **Merits that change the bond:** Unbondable (•••••, immune); Bond Resistance (•–•••, +1 die per level to resist); Bond Junkie (•, −1 die vs bonds); Short Bond (••, fade 2 levels/month); Long Bond (•, fade every 3 months); Bondslave (••, bond at one drink); Bonds of Fealty (•••, no eye contact needed for Dominate on own thralls); Enduring Bond (•, bonds created fade every other month); Two Masters (• flaw, be bound to two regnants at once). (Player's Guide / Blood-Stained Love / Gehenna War.)

### 2.4 Sabbat Vinculum (Vaulderie pack ritual)

The **Vaulderie** is the Sabbat ritual that replaces normal blood bonds among pack members. Source: VTM Wiki Sabbat; White Wolf Wiki Vaulderie; Guide to the Masquerade; V20 Sabbat book.

- All pack members contribute vitae to a communal chalice; all drink together. (Variants: Vaulderie of the Sword, the Vessel, etc.)
- **Effect 1:** The Vaulderie immediately **breaks any existing Blood Bonds** the participants had to non-pack Kindred (a critical recruitment tool — Kindred bound to a Camarilla elder can be freed by joining a Sabbat pack).
- **Effect 2:** A pack-wide **Vinculum** forms: each participant rolls **1d10** for each other participant, generating a Vinculum rating (1–10) toward that specific packmate.
- **Vinculum rating scale:** 1 = indifference; 2–4 = affection / mild loyalty; 5–7 = strong loyalty, will aid packmate; 8–9 = devoted; 10 = full blood-bond equivalent (will die for them).
- Vinculum ratings are **asymmetric** (A's rating to B differs from B's to A) and are **re-rolled every time the Vaulderie is performed**.
- Unlike a normal Blood Bond, **Vinculum does NOT fade with time** — only re-rolling can lower it (and rolling a 1 again lowers it; otherwise stays the same).
- Pack members who skip a Vaulderie keep their existing Vinculum ratings; new members joining the pack roll into the existing web.
- The Sabbat considers Vinculum sacred; refusing the Vaulderie is heresy.
- Alternative V5 optional rule: instead of d10, roll 1d6 + 4 to determine Vinculum strength on a 1–10 scale.

## 3. Coterie & Ties (V5 — "Blood Ties" merits)

**Source:** VTM Wiki Advantages & Flaws; V5 Gehenna War p.44 (Blood Ties introduced here); V5 Companion p.31–39 (Clan Coterie Merits).

### 3.1 The Three "Blood Ties" Merits

These are **three related Merits** under the **Blood Ties** category (page reference: Gehenna War p.44). They reflect the bond of vampiric lineage — between sire and childe, grandsire/grandchilde, or any Kindred of the same Clan. The site's task description ("Sense, Influence, Sins") maps directly to:

| Merit | Dots | Bonus / Effect |
|-------|------|----------------|
| **Consanguineous Sense** | •• | You can detect whether another Kindred is in your **direct bloodline** (sire, childe, grandsire, etc.). Does not tell you their Generation — only the relation. |
| **Consanguineous Influence** | •• | +1 die to Mental Discipline pools when targeting a vampire of your own Clan or a direct descendant/ancestor. **+2 dice** instead when targeting Kindred within two generations of you (e.g., your grandsire or grandchilde). |
| **Sins of the Father** | •• or ••• | You show no aura/stain signs if you commit **diablerie** on a direct descendant or ancestor. At three dots, this extends to **all members of your Clan** (i.e., you can diablerize any clanmate undetected). |

Note: Caitiff and Duskborn (thin-bloods) have lineages too, but their blood isn't cohesive enough to count as a "clan" for the purposes of these Merits — they may take the Merits but the bonuses are limited to direct sire/childe/grandsire relations only.

### 3.2 How they are chosen at coterie creation

These are not freebies tied to a coterie type — they are **standard Merits** purchased with the 7 starting Advantage dots (Corebook p.179). They are most often taken by **clan-coterie** configurations (e.g., a Ventrue-only or Banu Haqim-only coterie) because the bonuses only kick in when interacting with same-clan Kindred. Per the V5 Companion, Clan Coterie Merits (below) are distinct from Blood Ties — they are tied to the presence of a clanmate in the coterie (one member per session can use them).

## 4. Boons / Prestation Debts (Camarilla economy of favors)

**Source:** VTM V5 Corebook p.314–315; Players Guide p.173; VTM Wiki Boons.

### 4.1 What Prestation is

Boons are **supernaturally enforced promises** between Kindred. The system of recording and trading them is called **Prestation**. While raw power is the ultimate currency of vampire society, Kindred obtain power through the process of trading, repaying, and incurring favors. Boons are tracked by **Harpies** in Camarilla domains (extensive ledgers); Anarchs also honor their own boons as a mark of personal honor, refusing to need princes or courts to enforce them. There is **no way out of a boon**: it must be paid or formally excused by the holder.

### 4.2 V5 Boon Levels (Corebook p.314–315)

V5 uses **four** standard boon levels. (V20 used five — adding **Blood boon** between Major and Life; that older tier is preserved in the table for legacy/lore completeness.)

| Boon | V5 | Worth / Examples |
|------|----|--------------------|
| **Trivial** | ✓ | No risk/cost to the granter; might not even be tracked within a coterie. **Examples:** help find blood for the night; get an invitation to an exclusive nightclub; make space in a haven. |
| **Minor** | ✓ | Some effort — going out of one's way; some risks. **Examples:** vote in favor of another (only if minimal social harm); kill an unimportant human in the way; provide refuge to someone in dire need (sanctuary/sustenance); provide access to ancient tomes. |
| **Major** | ✓ | Not given lightly — significant risk and expense. **Examples:** grant rich hunting grounds to another; reveal a major secret; leverage own resources toward someone else's agenda; change one's expected vote in council. |
| **Blood boon** *(V20 / legacy)* | V20 only | A boon paid in **vitae** — typically granting a sip of one's blood (sometimes used to begin or fully form a Blood Bond). Worth between Major and Life in V20 society. **(Not a standard V5 tier — included for legacy lore.)** |
| **Life** | ✓ | Very rare; usually only when Final Death or the death of a Touchstone is on the horizon. **Examples:** save one's unlife desperately; save a touchstone's life; keep a deadly secret that would otherwise end the debtor. |

### 4.3 Boon classification is case-by-case

The same action can be a different-tier boon depending on the granter's circumstances — e.g., a major boon from a neonate may be a trivial boon from a Prince. Classification hinges on: fulfillment ability, circumstance, difficulty for the granter, desperation (the debtor "sweetening the deal" to get something immediately needed).

### 4.4 Prestation economy rules

- If the holder of a boon is killed, the boon **transfers** to the holder's sire or eldest childe (most domains); some domains transfer it to the Prince or Harpies upon death.
- Claiming to hold a boon when you don't, or **defaulting / refusing to pay**, can destroy a Kindred's reputation.
- **Coterie boons:** if a coterie holds or owes a boon, **any member** of the coterie may call in its use — or, conversely, suffer when a boon the coterie owes is called upon (Players Guide p.173).
- The Boon-owning Kindred keeps a +1 die bonus in **Social combat** against the debtor — even if the boon is paid off, this lingering social leverage persists (per Prestation Debts Flaw, Players Guide p.121).
- Harpies in Camarilla domains track who owes whom; some keep extensive public lists; owing too many boons makes a Kindred look weak.

## 5. Convictions & Touchstones (V5 Humanity system)

**Source:** VTM V5 Corebook p.236–240 (Humanity); p.240 (Touchstone destruction); VTM Wiki Humanity, Conviction, Touchstone; Seattle By Night convictions FAQ; Vampire Mashup Wiki; Onyx Path Forums (V5: Lost Convictions and Touchstones).

### 5.1 Overview

The V5 Humanity system is built on three interlocking layers:

1. **Chronicle Tenets** — a small set of moral rules agreed by the table, applying to **all** player characters regardless of personal belief. Breaking a Chronicle Tenet imposes Stains. Examples: "Thou shalt not kill, save in self-defense"; "Thou shalt not rape or torture"; "Never deny true love."
2. **Convictions** — 1–3 personal moral codes per vampire. They are the guidelines by which the vampire wishes to live their unlife. They are **not required to be morally good** — they are simply the vampire's own code (religious, ethical, philosophical, personal, or just habit).
3. **Touchstones** — each Conviction is paired to one Touchstone: a single **living human being** who represents what the vampire used to value in life. The Touchstone is the *anchor* of the Conviction.

Each vampire begins with **1 to 3 Convictions**, and an equal number of Touchstones.

### 5.2 How Convictions mitigate Stains

- **Stains** damage Humanity (fill the tracker from right to left). Causes of Stains: embracing a new childe, harming a touchstone, breaching a personal conviction, breaching a Chronicle Tenet.
- **Mitigation:** When a Chronicle Tenet is broken **in the service of (aligned with) a Conviction**, the character's Conviction **reduces the number of Stains gained by one or more**. Example: chronicle has a "no murder" tenet, but the character's Conviction is "Always act in aid of a coterie-mate" — a murder committed in aid of a coterie-mate might award **zero Stains**.
- **Awarding Stains:** Conversely, **violating a personal Conviction** can, at ST discretion, **incur one or more Stains** on top of any Chronicle Tenet Stains. A good conviction thus both **awards and mitigates** Stains, depending on whether it is followed or broken.
- Stains vary: 1 Stain for a clear violation; 2+ for truly monstrous/egregious breaches.

### 5.3 The Remorse test (end of session)

At the end of each session, any character with Stains in their tracker makes a **Remorse test**.

- **Pool:** Number of empty spaces between current Humanity and current Stains (a Humanity 7 character with 1 Stain rolls 2 dice; a Humanity 7 character with 3 Stains still rolls 1 die).
- **Success:** Any successes = the character feels enough guilt/shame/remorse; Humanity does not drop.
- **Failure (zero successes):** The Beast has won; Humanity drops by 1.
- After the Remorse test, **all Stains are removed** from the tracker (whether Humanity dropped or not).

### 5.4 Degeneration (overflow)

If a character takes more Stains than they have empty spaces:
- They become **impaired** (−2 dice from all pools, racked with regret).
- Take **1 point of Aggravated Willpower damage** per Stain that did not fit.
- Cannot commit further intentional Tenet violations; if forced, must test for **terror frenzy** at Difficulty 4.
- Impairment remains until the Remorse test at session end — or they may **snap out** by automatically dropping 1 Humanity and removing all Stains.

### 5.5 Touchstone destruction (V5 Corebook p.240)

When a Touchstone is killed, destroyed, permanently corrupted, or otherwise removed from the vampire's life:

- The vampire **loses the associated Conviction** immediately.
- The vampire suffers **automatic Stains** (typically 2 Stains for the loss).
- The vampire must make a Remorse test at the next session end (with the accumulated Stains).
- A **replacement Touchstone** (and Conviction) can be acquired **after one full story** of grief, soul-searching, or seeking a new anchor — at Storyteller's discretion. The replacement must be a new living human.
- A related Background flaw: **Touchstone Embraced by your Enemies** (••) — a former Touchstone didn't die but was Embraced by enemies; this is a single Neonate with allies, and confrontation risks Stains. (In Memoriam p.20.)

- "Only kill in self defense"

- "Don't take from the needy"
- "Do not lie"

- "Obey authority"

### 5.7 Touchstone as Willpower recovery

Touchstones also serve as one of the main ways PCs recover Willpower — interacting with them, protecting them, or upholding a Conviction in their presence can restore Superficial Willpower damage. Losing Touchstones thus has compounding mechanical cost: more Stains risk + slower Willpower recovery.

### 6.1 Golconda (V5 — via Loresheet)

**Source:** VTM Wiki Loresheets (p.389); V5 Player's Guide p.389; Strange Assembly "Top Loresheets"; Seattle By Night loresheets rulings; Cape Town by Night wiki.

Golconda is a legendary state of vampiric enlightenment in which a vampire masters their Beast and overcomes the Curse of Caine. In V5, Golconda is not a Discipline or Path — it's represented via the **Golconda Loresheet** (Player's Guide p.389).

### 6.2 Torpor (V5)

**Source:** VTM V5 Corebook p.223 (Torpor); p.224 (Blood Potency & torpor interaction); p.241 (Torpor length per Humanity); VTM Wiki Torpor.

**Definition:** Torpor is a state where a vampire is dead to their surroundings — reduced to the appearance of a shriveled corpse, unable to use Disciplines or react to stimuli. It is **not Final Death**: the vampire will wake when the period dictated by their Humanity rating expires.

#### 6.2.1 Three ways to enter Torpor

1. **Failing a Rouse Check at Hunger 5** when awakening.
2. **Taking enough Aggravated Health damage to fill the entire Health tracker.** The vampire will unconsciously attempt to heal 1 point of Aggravated damage each night; if they heal all damage they may wake — but the torpor period is dictated by Humanity regardless.
3. **Voluntary torpor.** (Hunger still increases each night during voluntary torpor.)

#### 6.2.2 Torpor duration (by Humanity rating — V5 Corebook p.241)

| Humanity | Torpor length |
|----------|---------------|
| 10 | (No torpor entry in corebook; effectively immune at H10) |
| 9 | 3 days |
| 8 | 1 week |
| 7 | 2 weeks |
| 6 | 1 month |
| 5 | 1 year |
| 4 | 1 decade |
| 3 | 5 decades |
| 2 | 1 century |
| 1 | 5 centuries |
| 0 | (Wassail — final Rötschreck frenzy; character becomes a wight under ST control) |

Low-Humanity vampires spend vastly longer in torpor than high-Humanity ones.

#### 6.2.3 Waking from Torpor

- Once the torpor period expires, the vampire may make a **wake test** when a potential victim is nearby — if successful, they awaken to feed.
- They must reach **Hunger 4** in order to fully awaken from torpor (feeding rouses them progressively).
- **Staking exception:** If a vampire is staked (not in torpor naturally), they remain in torpor only as long as the stake is in place. Once the stake is removed, they immediately awaken at **Hunger 5**.
- **Force-awakening:** A vampire in torpor may be awoken earlier than dictated by being fed enough vitae to slake 1 Hunger from another vampire whose **Blood Potency is higher** than their own.
- During torpor, **Hunger increases each night** as normal (so a vampire waking from a long torpor wakes very hungry).

#### 6.2.4 Blood Potency interaction with Torpor

- **Blood Potency decreases** while in torpor: drops 1 dot per 50 years spent in torpor (per the optional rule cited in Blood Potency wiki + forums).
- Older elders use torpor strategically to manage their Blood Potency (which is otherwise forced to rise with age per the Corebook table).
- A character in torpor for centuries wakes up functionally a neonate in experience but an elder by age — they must rebuild their Blood Potency by feeding actively.
